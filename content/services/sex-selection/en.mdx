The reliable scientific way to choose a baby's sex is ICSI with preimplantation genetic testing (PGT). The sex of each embryo is read from its sex chromosomes: XX for a girl and XY for a boy, and an embryo of the chosen sex is transferred to the uterus.

## How is it done?

1. **Ovarian stimulation and egg retrieval,** as in ICSI.
2. **Injecting the eggs** and culturing the embryos to day 5.
3. **A small biopsy from the outer layer** of each embryo.
4. **Chromosome analysis** with next-generation sequencing (NGS), which also detects an abnormal number of chromosomes.
5. **Transfer of a healthy embryo of the chosen sex** in a later cycle.

## Medical reasons for sex selection

Some genetic diseases mainly affect boys because they are linked to the X chromosome, such as hemophilia and Duchenne muscular dystrophy. In these cases, choosing the embryo's sex helps avoid passing on the disease.

## What to know

- **Sex is identified very accurately,** because it is read from the chromosomes themselves, but a pregnancy is not guaranteed.
- **The result depends on the number of healthy embryos:** there may not be a healthy embryo of the chosen sex in every attempt, and more than one cycle may be needed.
- **Other popular methods,** such as special diets or timing intercourse, have not been shown to determine the baby's sex.
- **The decision is discussed with the doctor,** including its medical and ethical sides, before starting.
