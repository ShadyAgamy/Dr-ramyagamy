Endoscopic surgery makes it possible to diagnose and treat many problems of the uterus, ovaries and tubes through very small incisions, or with no incision at all. A high-definition camera sends a magnified image to a screen, so the surgeon sees the details more clearly than in open surgery.

## Hysteroscopy

The scope goes in through the cervix, with no incision in the abdomen, and the patient usually goes home the same day. It is used to:

- Remove **polyps** inside the uterus.
- Remove **fibroids** that bulge into the uterine cavity.
- Treat a **uterine septum,** which can cause recurrent miscarriage.
- Release **adhesions** inside the uterus.
- Assess the uterine cavity in delayed pregnancy or after failed ICSI.

## Laparoscopy

It is done through small incisions in the abdomen, usually 5 to 10 millimeters. It is used to:

- Remove **ovarian cysts** while keeping as much healthy ovarian tissue as possible.
- Treat **endometriosis.**
- Remove **fibroids** from the wall of the uterus.
- Treat an **ectopic pregnancy.**
- Deal with **blocked or fluid-filled tubes** before ICSI.
- Check the **fallopian tubes with dye** during the procedure.

## The benefits of endoscopic surgery

- **Less pain** after the operation.
- **A shorter hospital stay,** sometimes with no overnight stay.
- **A faster return** to normal life and work.
- **Fewer adhesions** after surgery, which matters for fertility.
- **Better cosmetic results** thanks to the small incisions.

Not every case is suitable for endoscopy. The type of surgery is decided after examination, ultrasound and an assessment of each case.
