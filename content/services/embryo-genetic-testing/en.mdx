Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) is done on embryos created through ICSI before they are transferred to the uterus, to choose embryos that are genetically or chromosomally healthy.

## Types of testing

- **PGT-A:** detects an abnormal number of chromosomes, such as Down syndrome. This is one of the main causes of implantation failure and early miscarriage, especially as age rises.
- **PGT-M:** detects a specific genetic disease known in the family, such as thalassemia or spinal muscular atrophy.
- **PGT-SR:** for couples where one partner carries a structural chromosome change, such as a balanced translocation.

## How is it done?

1. **ICSI** to create the embryos.
2. **Embryo culture to day 5 or 6** (the blastocyst stage).
3. **A small biopsy of cells from the outer layer** of the embryo, the part that later forms the placenta, without touching the cells that form the baby itself.
4. **Freezing the embryos** by vitrification until the results are ready.
5. **Analysis of the samples** with modern techniques such as next-generation sequencing (NGS).
6. **Transfer of a healthy embryo** in a later cycle, after preparing the uterus.

## Who is it recommended for?

- Couples who carry a known genetic disease, or who have an affected child.
- Carriers of a structural chromosome change.
- Some cases of recurrent miscarriage or repeated implantation failure, after assessment.
- Some cases where the woman is older.

## What to know before starting

- Testing does not guarantee a pregnancy, but it helps choose the most suitable embryo.
- No test is 100% accurate. For genetic diseases, confirming the result during pregnancy is advised.
- Some embryos are "mosaic" and need a specific discussion before any decision.
- Genetic counseling before testing is recommended, to explain the possible results.
